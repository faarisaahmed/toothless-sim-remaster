# PART I — THE BIBLE

## 0. Frame

### 0.1 Continuity anchor

One month after Johann and Krogan are defeated and the Bewilderbeast — the King of
Dragons — swims free. The Riders have dismantled Dragon's Edge and come home to Berk.
The war is over. This story occupies the quiet year *before* the events of the second
film, and ends before it begins.

Consequences that the whole story rests on:

- **Toothless is not the alpha yet.** He is one dragon among many. This game is the
  hidden reason he is ready to take the title a year later, and the reason he doesn't
  hesitate when he does.
- **Peacetime.** Nobody on Berk is in danger. Nothing needs saving. That is the problem.
- **The vacuum.** Johann's operation — fleets, cages, crews, trained flyers, the whole
  industrial apparatus of dragon capture — did not die with Johann. A month is exactly
  how long it takes for someone to buy the wreckage.
- **The prototype fin.** Hiccup built an automatic tailfin, early and imperfect. It flies
  badly. He shelved it after this. Years later he gets it right.
- **He is still the only one.** Toothless has never met another Night Fury. Every new
  species he meets is a small no.

> **Continuity check pending:** verify whether Race to the Edge already put an automatic
> tailfin prototype on screen. If it did, inherit it — the fin already exists and Hiccup
> simply gives it back. If it didn't, this is where it's invented.

### 0.2 Logline

A month after the war ends, the dragon who won it discovers there is nothing left for him
to be — so he leaves alone, and finds a war nobody has noticed.

### 0.3 The swap layer

Everything in this bible sorts into two piles. Keep them separable.

**Load-bearing and original — survives any rename:**

- The post-war power vacuum as the villain's on-ramp
- Industrialized capture: cages, quotas, an assembly line rather than a raid
- A prosthetic built for two, worn by one, that fights its user
- Sleepfire: the involuntary purge that turns out to be a weapon
- The lab-on-an-island loop; the derived solution
- Peacetime purposelessness as the inciting emotion
- The metal made *from* the thing it cages

**Borrowed and disposable — will be filed off:**

- Every proper noun
- Species designs and their specific fire types
- The Viking dressing

When writing new material, put the weight on pile one. If a beat only works because of a
name, it's a beat that will die in the rewrite.

**The name pass — decided.** Do it now, as a table, not a rewrite. The prose below keeps
the borrowed names because they're readable and everyone already knows who's who. This
table is the **single point of change**, and nothing outside it needs touching.

Naming register to hold to: Vikings name dragons by one observed trait, plainly and a
little rudely. Two syllables, compound, no elves. If a new name sounds noble, it's wrong.

### 0.4 Theme

**Stated question:** what is a weapon for, once the war is over?

**Actual question:** can he be somebody without the boy?

The tailfin is the theme made of leather and iron. It is a gift, and it is a leash, and
both readings are true at once. He cannot fly without Hiccup's hands or Hiccup's
invention. The game never resolves this cleanly. It only moves it — from *dependence* to
*something chosen*.

The answer he arrives at is not "I don't need him." It's: **there are dragons out here
with nobody at all, and I know what that's like, and I can be that for them.** He goes
looking for a purpose and finds a responsibility, which is what a purpose actually is.

### 0.5 Tone and rating

**Kids can play this.** That's a design constraint, and a productive one.

- **Nobody dies.** Not humans, not dragons, not offscreen. The tail-strike is explicitly a
  pressure-point knockout and the game shows people getting back up later.
- **No blood, no bodies, no burning people.** When the rig goes down, we cut to the crews
  getting into boats and being *fine*, visibly, on purpose.
- **Halvard is never killed and never beaten up.** He loses the industry. For a man whose
  whole self is his work, that's the harsher ending anyway.
- **The threat is capture, not death** — cages, muzzles, nets, and a life spent being
  useful to someone. That's frightening at any age and it needs no gore to land.
- **What we don't soften:** loneliness, failure, being caught, and the fact that some
  things you break don't fully mend. Bellows never flies right again. Children can handle
  sad. They cannot handle being condescended to.

## 1. Cast

### Toothless — the player

Not a hero yet. A veteran. He has been in a war since he was young and he was extremely
good at it, and now it's over and he is twenty-two hours of every day asleep on a warm
stone in someone else's house.

He is clever in a way nobody has ever asked him to be alone. He watches Hiccup solve
problems constantly. He has absorbed the method — test, observe, revise — without ever
having had a reason to run it himself. The lab sequence is the first time in his life he
does science, and he is *good at it*, and nobody is there to see.

**Voice:** none. No narration, no thought bubbles, no subtitled growls. Everything he
feels is posture, ears, pupils, tail. The rig is the script.

**Flaw:** he solves things by going in alone. It nearly kills him in Mission 1 and it is
the thing the whole game is about unlearning.

### Hiccup — the absence

Present in the prologue and the epilogue and nowhere in between. Not a mentor, not a
voice on the radio. His absence has to be *felt*, mechanically: the fin flies worse
without him, the plan has no second opinion, there is no one to sit with after.

He is not neglectful. He's nineteen, he's chief-in-training, and he thinks the hard part
of his life is over. That's the tragedy of the prologue — he's *happy*, and he has no
idea his best friend is lying awake.

### The Stormcutter — Sigrún (placeholder)

A four-winged adult on a lonely stack, with a broken wing and a hatchling.

**She has nothing to do with the rig.** She has never seen it, never been hunted, never
lost anyone to it. She broke her wing in a storm two weeks ago and has been slowly
starving ever since, because a dragon that can't fly can't fish. She is an ordinary
dragon having an ordinary catastrophe.

**This is the most important thing about her.** She has no stake, no grievance, and
nothing to avenge. Every reason she could have for helping him is one we deliberately
took away. What's left is the only reason that matters.

She does not trust him, and shouldn't — he is an enormous stranger who arrived at speed.

Her arc across Mission 1 is four points:

1. He feeds her and leaves. She does not thank him.
2. She watches him from a distance while he plans. The player should notice her and not
   be able to interact.
3. **She follows him to the rig.** Uninvited, unasked, wing barely mended, and she leaves
   the hatchling nested and alone on the stack to do it — because she wants to know what
   this strange dragon is *for*.
4. From the sky, she watches him choose the last cage over the open exit. *That* is the
   moment. Not the fish.

She is the game's proof of thesis: he doesn't earn a follower by being strong, or by being
owed. He earns one by being seen doing the tactically wrong thing for the right reason,
by someone who had every excuse to stay out of it.

**After the rescue she doesn't go home and wait.** She decides, on her own, that this is
work worth doing and that he is manifestly not equipped to do it alone. **From Mission 2
she is a permanent partner** — the game becomes a two-hander, and his arc stops being
"learn to lead" and becomes the harder, more specific "learn to work with someone."

### The hatchling — Eyvi (placeholder)

Too young to fly properly. Functions as a walking liability and as the reason the sleepfire
lesson lands — he watches *her* sleep-purge before he understands he does it too. A
hatchling's purge is tiny and harmless and she does it three times a night, which is
exactly why he never noticed adults doing it.

### The antagonist — Halvard Sten (placeholder)

**Not a brute.** An engineer, a metallurgist, and Hiccup's dark mirror in the most
uncomfortable way: he is *also* the smartest person in his world, he *also* believes
dragons are extraordinary, and he *also* built his life around a workshop. He simply drew
a different conclusion from the same evidence.

He does not hate dragons. He is not cruel for pleasure. He finds them the most
remarkable engineering problem of the age and he intends to *solve* them. He speaks about
his captives with real, appalling warmth.

**His edge:** he bought Johann's infrastructure a month ago, at a discount, from people
desperate to be rid of it. He inherited an empire and immediately started improving it,
because his predecessors were amateurs who thought of dragons as cargo.

**The metal.** Dragon-proof alloy is his invention and his fortune. He sells it. Every
hunter in the archipelago will be buying it within a year. This is why he is more
dangerous than Johann ever was: Johann wanted dragons, Halvard wants *the industry*.

**The secret (Mission 2 reveal):** the alloy requires a heat no forge can reach. He has a
dragon in the core of the rig producing it. The cages that hold dragons are made by a
dragon. The rig gets stronger every single day the player does not act.

He should never fight Toothless directly. He watches. He takes notes. When Toothless
escapes in Mission 1, Halvard's reaction is not rage — it's *fascination*, and an order
to redesign the cages.

**His ending:** he is never killed and never beaten. He loses the works, the alloy, the
supply, and the whole industry he was a year from owning. He walks away from the wreck
alive, unhurt, and *ruined*, and for a man who is entirely his work that is the worse
outcome. He'd have preferred the fight.

### Bellows — the forge dragon

A Kilnback. Enormous, ancient, and the source of every piece of dragon-proof alloy in the
world. The crew named them after a tool and nobody has ever called them anything else.

**They are not chained.** They were, years ago. The chains came off a long time back
because they stopped being necessary — Bellows doesn't leave. This is the job. They are
*good at it*, they are the only one who can do it, and being needed is the only thing
they have ever been given.

**They are dying of not sleeping.** This is the reveal that ties the whole game together:
a dragon worked at smelting heat never drops into deep sleep, so they never purge, so
decades of residue have packed their glands solid. Bellows is sick with exactly the thing
the player learned to use. **The mechanic at the centre of the game is also the injury at
the centre of the villain's business.**

**And Halvard is retiring them.** He says it kindly, and he means it kindly, and he has
already got a younger dragon picked out for the chain. That's the clock: not a bomb, not a
countdown — a replacement, arriving.

**The finale is putting someone to bed.** Not breaking a chain. Not a boss. The player has
to get a dragon who has not properly slept in twenty years to lie down, in the dark, in
safety, and let go — and the purge that follows is twenty years deep and takes the smelter
apart from the inside.

**They survive and they do not recover.** Bellows never smelts again, never flies far, and
lives out their life on Hollow Stack in the sun, which is more than they have ever had and
less than they should have got.

### The rig — a character

Not a fortress on an island. A fortress *of* iron built across and between three sea
stacks: a smelter, a cage tier, a dock, and a spine of walkways strung between them over
open water. It never stops. Furnace glow visible from a long way off at night. Sound
before sight.

Its size should be established by *flying past it once*, low, at night, and having it not
end.

## 2. Systems that carry story

Every one of these exists because a story beat needs it, and every story beat is playable
because one of these exists. That is the contract.

### 2.1 The fin

Three states across the game:

| State | Feel | When |
|---|---|---|
| **Ridden** | Smooth, responsive, effortless | Prologue only. Establishes the baseline he loses. |
| **Prototype, solo** | Twitchy. Overcorrects on roll. Drifts in a sustained turn. Stalls if you climb too steep. | The entire game. |
| **Tuned** | Not fixed — *learned*. Same fin, but the player has adapted, and Toothless has made physical adjustments with his teeth. | Late, earned, gradual. |

The prototype's specific defect matters: it was built assuming a rider would correct it.
Flying solo, it fights him. This is diegetic difficulty, and it means the player's growing
competence at the flight model *is* the character's arc. Do not "upgrade" it with a menu.
Let the player get good and then, once, have Toothless bite a strap tighter and have it
help by a measurable amount.

### 2.2 Sleepfire — the spine

**The mechanism.** A dragon's fire glands accumulate residue. To stay serviceable they
purge involuntarily during deep sleep — a single, enormous, unaimed discharge, far hotter
than anything a waking dragon can produce. It lands on the dragon's own fireproof hide
and dissipates. Every dragon has done this every night of its life and no dragon has ever
thought about it, because it happens while they are asleep.

Toothless learns it exists by *doing it onto a sample of Halvard's alloy* and waking up
next to a hole in it.

**The rules:**

- **He can produce it awake.** Barely. It is not a breath — it comes from lower, and it
  hurts. Input should reflect this: a long, held, uncomfortable action, not a trigger pull.
- **It costs.** Rest and food, spent in advance. A sleepfire on an empty stomach fails and
  leaves him staggering. The hunger/rest economy is not survival-game busywork; it is the
  ammunition counter.
- **It is loud and bright.** In a stealth mission, firing it is a decision that changes the
  level. Every guard on the tier turns around.
- **One species, one fire.** He can only produce his own.

**The Metroidvania spine.** Every species purges differently — Stormcutter fire spirals
and *clings*; a Gronckle's is molten and pools; a Zippleback's hangs as gas and waits.
Toothless can't produce them. But he can **sleep beside a dragon that can**, and in the
morning he understands something new about what fire is for.

This means: *befriending a species is acquiring a key.* No gear pickups, no upgrade tree,
no shops. The progression system is entirely relationships. A door you can't open in
Mission 2 opens in Mission 5 because of who is asleep next to you.

It also means the game's collectibles are *sleeping next to people*, which is the whole
theme wearing a mechanic's coat.

### 2.3 The lab

A flat rock shelf in the shelter. Three axes:

- **Fire:** plasma burst / sustained plasma / low smoulder / (later) borrowed species fires
- **Material:** the alloy sample, plus iron, rope, stone, ice, wood, sailcloth
- **Condition:** cold / pre-heated / soaked / sustained-over-time / repeated-burst

**The one rule: the player derives, never looks it up.** There is no recipe list, no
tooltip that says *weak to fire*. There is a wall, and on the wall are the scratches you
made recording what happened. Failures stay on the wall permanently. The wall *is* the
quest journal.

**The tempting wrong answer.** Early on, sustained plasma against pre-heated alloy
produces a visible deformation — a dent, a colour change. It looks like progress. A player
will absolutely commit to this line and pour a night into it. It plateaus. It never cuts
through. This is essential: the sleep discovery has to arrive after genuine failure or it's
a gift instead of a reward.

**Persistence.** The lab is a hub. Later missions bring back new samples — hull plate,
chain, a cage hinge, the treated rope they use for muzzles — and the wall accumulates for
the whole game. Between story missions you come back here. This is the Spider-Man cadence
and it's the right one.

### 2.4 Dragon-speech

No words. A short verb set the player performs with the rig:

- **Chirp** — get attention, low volume
- **Roar** — get attention, very high volume, wrong tool for stealth
- **Nudge / head-press** — trust, calm, "follow me"
- **Bare teeth / low growl** — warning, "stay back"
- **Tail-point** — direction. "Go *there*."

Different dragons have different comprehension. The clever ones (Stormcutters,
Zipplebacks) parse tail-point and follow it. Panicked ones only respond to head-press.
A hatchling responds to nothing reliably.

This is the system that makes the Mission 1 climax work: the player will *try* to tell the
hatchling to be quiet, and the verb set will simply not contain a way to do it.

### 2.5 Rest, food, and the day

Not a survival sim. Three coarse states — **Fed / Thin / Empty** — that gate sleepfire and
sprint. Fish exist in specific waters. Hunting is a competent, satisfying, thirty-second
verb, not a chore.

Sleep is the save. Sleeping advances to the next day, restores rest, and — critically —
is how you acquire species fires. The player will come to associate *going to sleep* with
*progress*, which is a lovely thing to teach.

Night is when the fortress is workable and when the sky is his. Day is when the world is
alive and the lab makes sense. Time-of-day is not cosmetic; it's the mission structure.

### 2.6 Stealth verbs

He has no tools. Everything is his body:

- **Wing-gust** — a downstroke that snuffs torches and braziers at range. Creates the
  darkness he needs. Also rattles loose sailcloth, which can be heard.
- **Tail-strike, neck** — the pressure-point knockout. Silent, non-lethal, requires a
  full approach from behind. If it half-lands, they shout.
- **Silent glide** — the Night Fury's actual signature. Powered flight is audible; a glide
  from height is not. Managing altitude *is* managing noise.
- **Perch** — cling to a wall, a beam, an underside. Vertical space is the stealth
  playfield; the rig should be built with a full ceiling of places to hang.
- **Plasma blast** — useless on the alloy, excellent on rope, wood, and lanterns. Learning
  what it is *for* now that it isn't for winning fights is a small nice arc of its own.

## 3. MISSION 1 — "The Metal and the Dark"

Full beat sheet. **Cutscene budget: three.** They are marked ★. Everything else is played.

---

### Scene 1 — The Room *(playable, interior, night)*

**Where:** Hiccup's house. Toothless's stone slab, still warm. Hiccup asleep across the
room. Low light — hearth embers and one shuttered window.

**Camera:** low and close. This is the only time in the game the camera is inside a
building, and it should feel *small*, because the rest of the game is enormous.

**What the player does:** almost nothing, slowly. He can't sleep. Movement is a heavy,
quiet pad around a room. Objects can be looked at, and looking is the whole scene:

| Object | What it gives |
|---|---|
| **The saddle** | On its stand. Unused for eleven days. He looks a long time. |
| **The prototype fin** | On the floor in a corner, half under a bench, dusty. Hiccup made it, it flew badly, they laughed about it, it went in the corner. |
| **Hiccup, asleep** | He can nudge him. Hiccup mumbles, shifts, doesn't wake. Toothless backs off. This should hurt. |
| **The map on the wall** | Hiccup's chart of the archipelago, edges blank. The blank part is the game. |
| **The window shutter** | He can push it open with his nose. Wind, sea, dark. He does not leave yet. |

No objective marker. No prompt telling you to look at things. The scene ends when the
player lies back down on the slab — which is the only obvious thing to do in a room at
night, and doing it is a small act of resignation.

**Then he sleeps, and thinks.** Not a dream — a drift. Three fragments, each three to
six seconds, each a *flyable* half-second of muscle memory: a dive he did in the war, the
Bewilderbeast turning away, Hiccup's hand on the fin lever. They don't resolve. They stop.

**Design note:** the fragments are the only place we ever show the war, and we show it as
something his body remembers rather than something he misses.

### Scene 2 — Morning ★ *(cutscene, ~40 seconds)*

Grey dawn. Hiccup is already gone — there's noise from the village, the shape of a busy
day happening without him.

Toothless walks to the corner, and pulls the prototype fin out from under the bench with
his teeth.

The last shot is him working out the buckles alone, which takes an embarrassingly long
time, and getting it wrong twice, and getting it.

No music swell. He is not setting off on an adventure. He is a person deciding to go do
something rather than lie there.

---

### Scene 3 — First Solo *(playable, tutorial, morning)*

The flight model tutorial, disguised as a man learning to walk again.

He launches from the cliff behind the house and it goes **badly** — the fin overcorrects,
he drops thirty feet, he catches it. The player learns the handling by having it be
genuinely difficult in a moment where failure costs nothing but dignity.

**Objective:** none. Fly. Stay up. The tutorial is the terrain.

**Soft gate:** the boundary of the mapped archipelago. Every island here is named, known,
on Hiccup's chart. Crossing out of it is not a prompt or a confirm dialog. The named
islands simply run out, and the water past them is a different colour, and the chart in
your head has nothing on it.

**When he crosses:** no fanfare. The music, if any, stops. That's it.

---

### Scene 4 — Open Water *(playable, exploration, day → dusk)*

The first real stretch of unstructured play. Empty sea, weather, distance. Long enough to
be slightly uncomfortable. He is looking for other dragons and there aren't any, which is
its own quiet piece of information.

**What's out here:**

- Two or three uninhabited stacks, one with a wrecked ship on it
- Fishing waters — the hunt verb is introduced here, unprompted, by there being fish
- A drifting spar of wood with **iron fittings that are the wrong colour** — first physical
  trace of the alloy, and it is scenery, not a pickup. Most players will fly past it.
- Weather that gets worse as he goes further out

---

### Scene 5 — The Rig *(playable, recon, night)*

He hears it before he sees it. Then furnace-glow on the underside of the cloud.

**This scene has exactly one instruction: do not be seen.** There is no combat option and
the game does not say so.

**Beat 5a — the pass.** Fly past it once, low, along its length. It does not end. Three
sea stacks, iron strung between them, a smelter throwing orange light, a cage tier, a
dock, patrol lanterns moving on the walkways. Scale established by duration.

**Beat 5b — the cages.** Perch on a spar above the tier. Look down. Dragons, dozens,
muzzled, in cages. This is a long look with no interaction available and the player will
want an interaction and there isn't one.

**Beat 5c — the failure.** He takes a shot at a cage bar. It's the only thing he knows
how to do.

The plasma blast hits, flares, and **does nothing.** Not "chips it." Nothing. The bar is
faintly scorched.

The blast is *heard*. Lanterns turn. He runs. The escape from the rig is the scene's
action beat and it should be genuinely frightening — bolas, nets, the walkways lighting up
behind him.

**What the player leaves with:** the certainty that the direct approach is not available,
arrived at by trying it. And, snatched off the tier on the way out, **a piece of the
alloy.**

---

### Scene 6 — The Island *(playable, hub establishment, night → day)*

Somewhere far enough away. He picks it — the game should offer three or four candidate
islands and let the choice be real, because this becomes home for the rest of the game.

**Building the shelter:** a short, tactile construction sequence with dragon verbs.
Dragging deadfall. Blasting a rock face to open an overhang. Scraping out a hollow.
Arranging a sleeping stone. It should take a while and be satisfying and involve no menu.

**The result:** a hub with a mouth facing the sea, a sleeping stone, a flat shelf that
will become the lab, and a wall.

---

### Scene 7 — The Lab *(playable, core puzzle, full day into night)*

He puts the alloy fragment on the shelf and starts.

**Structure:** open experimentation, no goal state given. Fire × material × condition. The
wall records everything with a claw-scratch. Time passes visibly — light moves across the
mouth of the cave, the light goes orange, then blue, then gone.

**The tempting wrong line:** pre-heat the alloy, then sustained plasma. It deforms
visibly. A dent, a bloom of colour. It is the *only* thing in the whole matrix that
produces any change at all, so the player will chase it. It plateaus at "slightly bent"
and no amount of iteration gets further.

**Design intent:** the player must run out of ideas. Not be interrupted — *run out*. The
scene ends when the experiments stop producing new information and the only remaining verb
is to lie down.

**He falls asleep beside the shelf,** with the dented sample where it was.

---

### Scene 8 — The Dream *(playable, ~2 minutes, surreal)*

**This is the best scene in the mission and it must not be a cutscene.**

A soft, hazy, sound-dampened space. No terrain, no horizon, no HUD. The player is still
in control but everything is slow and heavy.

**The player is the fire.** Not Toothless — the thing inside him. Movement is a slow
pressure moving upward through a dark warm space. It is uncomfortable and it is not
frightening. Controls are wrong on purpose: sluggish, resistant, more like pushing than
flying.

Fragments float past: the hatchling he hasn't met yet (a *hint*, not a flashback — he'll
recognise it in Scene 10 and so will the player), the furnace on the rig, Hiccup's hands,
the dented sample.

The pressure builds until it has nowhere to go, and releases.

**Cut to black. Sound of something cooling.**

---

### Scene 9 — Wake ★ *(cutscene, ~25 seconds)*

Morning light. He wakes badly, disoriented, mouth tasting of something.

The shelf is scorched black in a wide splash.

And in the middle of it, the alloy sample has **a hole through it.**

He stares at it. Turns it over. Puts his eye to the hole. Looks at the sun through it.

**No dialogue, no realisation music. Just a dragon holding up a piece of metal with a hole
in it, understanding something enormous.**

---

### Scene 10 — Fire *(playable, skill acquisition)*

He now has to do on purpose what he did asleep, and it is *hard*.

**The learning loop:**

- First attempts: nothing. He gags. Nothing comes.
- He works out it comes from lower than the throat — a different, uncomfortable place.
- The input is a long hold with a rising, unstable feedback. Release too early: fizzle.
  Hold too long: he chokes and takes a moment to recover.
- **First success is small.** A short, brutally hot gout that scorches a rock and drops him
  on his side, exhausted.
- Second attempt fails entirely — because he's spent.

**And that's the lesson.** Not "you unlocked an ability." He learns it costs rest and it
costs food, by having it not work when he's out of both. He goes fishing because the game
has made him *want* to, not because a quest said so.

**The hunt sequence** that follows should be a genuine pleasure — a good verb, good water,
good weather, and the first time in the game he looks content.

---

### Scene 11 — The Hatchling *(playable, encounter)*

A cry across the water while he's eating.

**Beat 11a — the flight.** Fast, urgent, no navigation aid, following sound. Let the
player lose the sound and have to circle back.

**Beat 11b — what he finds.** A Stormcutter adult, grounded, one wing badly broken and
splinted by nothing — *a storm did this, and the player should be able to read that from
the state of the stack around her: torn scrub, a fresh scar of rockfall, no rope, no nets,
no people.* A hatchling beside her, starving, and too young to hunt.

**No rig connection. None.** Resist every temptation to give her a grievance.

**Beat 11c — the approach.** She threatens him. The player must use the dragon-speech
verbs and get it wrong at least once. Roar is available and roaring here is a mistake.

**Beat 11d — fish.** Several trips. This is a chore and it should be, slightly — the point
is that he does it repeatedly, without acknowledgement, and leaves.

**Beat 11e — the purge.** That night, camped a respectful distance away, he watches the
hatchling sleep, and sees her do a tiny sleepfire. A puff. Harmless. She does it three
times.

**That's the *why* he never knew.** Adults do it enormously and privately and it lands on
their own hide. A hatchling does it constantly and visibly. He has now seen from the
outside what he did from the inside.

**Beat 11f — leaving.** He leaves in the morning. She does not thank him. There is no
quest completion. No companion joins the party.

---

### Scene 12 — The Plan *(playable, the recon board)*

**This is the scene that replaces "he decides to wing it."**

Back at the shelter. A patch of flat sand at the cave mouth. Everything he saw at the rig
in Scene 5 is available as marks he can drag into place with a claw:

- Patrol lanterns and their timings
- Torch and brazier positions
- The cage tier
- The dock, the smelter, the walkway spine
- Wind direction
- Approach vectors and an exit

**The player builds the actual route.** Not a menu of three plans — a physical board where
you place your entry point, order your cage-opening sequence, and pick your exit. What you
build is what the mission loads.

**Optional recon runs.** If the player wants better information they can fly out and
observe more. This is voluntary and it directly improves the board. Skipping it is a valid,
riskier choice.

**The plan is wrong in exactly one way, and it must be a way the player could not have
known:** the guard rotation the player observed was a *night* rotation, and on the night of
the raid a supply ship is in at the dock, so the dock crew is awake and there are twice as
many bodies on the lower spine. Not the player's fault. Not a gotcha. Just: the world is
bigger than your information about it.

**This is what triggers the alarm later, and it is not the hatchling's fault.**

---

### Scene 13 — The Raid *(playable, stealth, night — the mission's centrepiece)*

Long. Twenty to thirty minutes. Full stealth sandbox.

**Phase 1 — Entry.** Silent glide from height, no powered flight, into whatever entry the
player chose. Wing-gust the perimeter braziers on the way in to make a corridor of dark.

**Phase 2 — The tier.** Guards on patrol. Tail-strike knockouts from behind, one at a
time, bodies left where they fall. Perching in the ceiling space between passes.

**Phase 3 — The cages.** Sleepfire opens them, and it is *expensive*:

- Every use is loud and bright — the tier reacts, patrols converge, the player has to
  break line of sight and wait it out
- Every use costs stores. There are more cages than there is fuel.
- **So the player must choose an order,** and choosing is the scene. The clever dragons
  first, because they'll follow the tail-point and get out clean. The panicked ones need a
  head-press and time you don't have.

**Phase 4 — Herding.** Freed dragons are not automatically saved. Tail-point sends the
smart ones out along the dark route. Some don't understand. Some won't leave. One
Zippleback keeps circling back. Managing an escape is harder than causing one.

**Phase 5 — The supply ship.** The unplanned crew. The dock is awake. The player's route
runs straight through it because that's the route they drew, and now there are lanterns
where the board said dark.

**The alarm goes up.** Because of the ship. Because of the plan. Because information is
always incomplete.

---

### Scene 14 — The Choice *(playable, the mission's whole point)*

Alarm. Chaos. Freed dragons scattering into the dark.

**The game shows you the way out.** Explicitly, generously, unambiguously — the exit route
lights up, it's clean, it's open, it works. Every piece of design language in the scene
says *go*.

**And there is one cage left, on the tier behind you, with a hatchling in it, screaming.**

That's it. That's the scene. The player has an exit and a cage and no time.

**Design requirements, non-negotiable:**

- **The player must actually choose.** No timer that decides for them. No cutscene that
  takes the controls. If they fly out, the game has to be prepared to handle it — and it
  should, by having Toothless turn back *anyway* at the mouth of the exit, out of the
  player's hands, in the only moment in the game where he overrides you. That's not a
  failure to give agency; that's a character with a will, and it should be *shocking*.
- The exit must be genuinely viable. If it's obviously a trap, the choice is fake.
- **The Stormcutter is in the sky above the rig and she sees him turn around.** Do not cut
  to her. Do not telegraph her. She is a shape in the dark that a second viewing will
  reveal. This is the payoff of Scene 11: she doesn't come back for the fish, she comes
  back because she watched him do the tactically wrong thing for the right reason.

**He gets the hatchling out of the cage. And then he's caught.** Nets, bolas, weight. The
capture should be fast, mechanical, and unglamorous — no boss fight, just industrial
equipment doing exactly what it was built to do.

**Last shot before the cage door:** Halvard walking over, unhurried, and looking at him
with the delight of a man who has found an unusually good specimen.

---

### Scene 15 — The Cage *(playable, ~90 seconds, confined)*

Not a cutscene. **The player is in the cage.**

Restricted movement, camera locked close, alloy bars, muzzle. The verbs available are:
thrash, bite the lock, and nothing. All of them fail. Sleepfire is unavailable — he is
spent, and the muzzle is on.

**Let this go on slightly too long.**

Outside, through the bars, in the dark: shapes moving. Shouting. A lantern goes out. Then
another.

---

### Scene 16 — Sigrún ★ *(cutscene, ~20 seconds)*

The cage roof comes apart.

Four wings above the rig. A Stormcutter should look *impossible* from below, and this is
the shot the whole species design exists for.

She tears the muzzle off with her teeth, without gentleness, and she does not look at him
kindly. She looks at him the way you look at someone whose stupidity you have decided to
take responsibility for.

**And she flew here on a wing that isn't mended, and she left her hatchling alone on a
rock in the middle of the sea to do it.** Neither of them says that. Both of them know it.
It's why she's angry.

---

### Scene 17 — The Last Cages *(playable, co-op finale, night)*

**The systems change.** She's with him now, and the level is designed around two:

- She opens what he can't reach; he opens what she can't fit to
- Her sleepfire — spiralling, clinging — does something his doesn't, and this is the
  **first key acquisition in the game**, demonstrated before it's ever explained
- She can carry dragons out that can't fly. He can't.
- Guards who see her *run*, and he can use that

**The finale is a run, not a fight.** Every remaining cage, then out, over the water.

**The rig doesn't burn — it stops.** Walkways down, cranes fouled, the smelter doused and
going dark, and forty dragons leaving through the roof of it. Behind them, in the water,
the crews are getting into boats, and the camera makes a point of showing that they're
fine. Nobody drowns. Nobody burns. The place is *broken*, not destroyed, and Halvard will
have it running again in a month, which is the whole reason there's a Mission 2.

He never wins a fight in this mission and that's right.

---

### Scene 18 — After *(playable, quiet, dawn)*

Back at his island. Dragons everywhere — the ones who followed him home, milling on the
beach, unsure. Sigrún on a rock, wing still bad. The hatchlings asleep in a heap.

The player can walk among them. Nothing is required. Some will let him near, some won't.

**The last beat:** he goes into the shelter, alone, and puts the alloy sample with the hole
in it on the wall. Beside it, he scratches a mark.

The wall has one entry on it. There is a great deal of wall left.

**End of Mission 1.**

## 4. Beyond Mission 1

### Act structure

**ACT I — TWO (Missions 1–3)**
He learns he can do this, and immediately learns he can't do it alone.
- **M2 — The Forge.** The rig is running again. They go back for answers about the alloy
  and find Bellows at the core — unchained, unwell, and unwilling to leave. **The mission
  fails because the door they need to open is the dragon's own mind**, and neither of them
  has any idea how to do that yet. They have to fly away and leave someone behind who
  didn't want to be rescued. That's the act break, and it's a better failure than a locked
  door.
- **M3 — Word Travels.** Dragons are arriving at Hollow Stack because word is spreading,
  and he is not remotely equipped for that. A rescue goes wrong because he runs it the way
  he ran Mission 1 — alone, out of habit, without telling her. **A dragon is retaken.** Not
  killed — retaken, and the player sees them in a cage two missions later, which is worse
  and lasts longer. Sigrún is not sad about this. She is *furious*, and she's right.

**ACT II — A WING (Missions 4–7)**
He learns to work with someone. Co-op verbs open up, the island becomes a base, and the
sleepfire keys accumulate through relationships.
- Species recruitment missions, each one a key and each one a night spent sleeping beside
  somebody new
- Halvard escalating: alloy sold to rival hunters, a second rig, dragon-proof *armour*
- **The mirror mission:** they reach a hunter operation and find it already wrecked — by
  dragons, and not carefully. Ships stove in, crews driven into the water, cages torn open
  and the freed dragons scattered with no one to guide them. Nobody is dead and everybody
  is terrified, humans and dragons both. He is not the only dragon who worked out he could
  fight back, and the other one didn't stop to ask what happens next. **First serious
  question about what he's building** — and the first time Sigrún is the one who says the
  thing he doesn't want to hear.

**ACT III — THE OFFER (Missions 8–10)**
- Halvard finds the island. Not by force — he's simply *there* when they get home, sitting
  calmly among the dragons, having touched none of them.
- **The offer:** he has studied the fin. He can build a better one. A real tail — alloy,
  articulated, permanent. Full autonomy, no straps, no boy, no rider. He can do it
  tomorrow. In exchange he wants only to keep studying. *He is not lying, and it is a
  genuinely good offer.*
- Toothless should visibly, seriously consider it. The player should feel the pull.
- **The refusal** is not "I don't need it." It's that the alloy in that tail would have to
  come out of Bellows, and he has spent the whole game unable to stop thinking about a
  dragon who has not slept in twenty years.
- **The clock:** Halvard is retiring Bellows, kindly, and has a younger dragon picked out
  for the job. There's no bomb and no timer — just a replacement, arriving.
- **Finale — "Lie Down."** Not a boss fight. The two of them get into the core, and the
  entire climax is **convincing a dragon who believes they are needed that they are
  allowed to stop.** Dragon-speech, every verb the game has taught, and Sigrún doing the
  part he can't — because she's a parent and he isn't.
  Bellows lies down. Bellows sleeps. **Twenty years of purge comes up at once** and takes
  the smelter apart from the inside while the two of them drag them clear.
  The player's final action in the game is putting someone to bed.

**EPILOGUE — Berk**
He comes home. Hiccup is delighted, and has no idea, and never will. Toothless has no way
to explain any of it and does not try.

The wing stays out there. Sigrún holds Hollow Stack — an island of dragons that has
nothing to do with humans, with Bellows asleep in the sun on the high rock and Eyvi, much
bigger now, flying badly around them. **He goes back and forth for the rest of his life.**
Berk is where he sleeps. That is where he works.

Final shot: Toothless on the warm stone in the corner of the room, and the fin — worn,
repaired, chewed, adjusted a hundred times — set down beside him. He fits it now.

*A year later, a Bewilderbeast will look him in the eye and he will not blink.*

### Sleepfire key table (draft)

| Species | Purge behaviour | Opens |
|---|---|---|
| Night Fury | Concentrated, drilling, extreme point-heat | Alloy plate, locks, bars |
| Stormcutter | Spirals, clings, burns for a long time | Sustained burns; heat-jam mechanisms |
| Gronckle | Molten, pools, flows downhill | Seams, floors, anything below |
| Zippleback | Gas that hangs, ignites later | Delayed detonations; area denial |
| Thunderdrum | Concussive rather than thermal | Shatters brittle alloy at depth; underwater |
| Whispering Death | Bores, and *tunnels* | Vertical routes into sealed places |

Each is a lock-and-key AND a relationship AND a night spent sleeping beside somebody.

## 5. Cutscene doctrine

**Budget: three per mission. Hard cap.** Mission 1 spends them on the departure, the
wake, and the cage roof coming apart.

**Rules:**

1. **If he could be doing it, he's doing it.** The dream is played. The cage is played.
   The choice is played. The rescue's *aftermath* is played.
2. **Never take the controls to show competence.** Only ever to show something happening
   *to* him.
3. **No dialogue anywhere except the prologue and epilogue.** Halvard talks; nobody
   answers him. Dragons never get subtitles.
4. **A cutscene is under 45 seconds** or it's a scene that should have been playable.
5. **The camera never explains.** If the audience needs to be told, the level was built
   wrong.

# PART II — THE BUILDABLE CUT

What this repo can actually ship, given: three.js, a solo dev, no animators, no VO, no
level artists, and a rigged Toothless.

## 6. What already exists

| File | Ships as |
|---|---|
| `js/world.js` | The archipelago. Named islands, height field, sky, water. Story world, already done. |
| `js/controls.js`, `js/gamepad.js`, `js/dualsense.js` | The flight model. **This is the fin.** Nothing to build. |
| `js/map.js` | Parchment chart traced from the real height field. **This becomes the plan board.** |
| `js/flights.js` | Wild dragon flights — becomes ambient dragon life and later the freed wing. |
| `js/wings.js`, `js/debug.js` | Rig control, tuning console. |
| `assets/models/toothless_rigged.glb` | The character. |
| `js/game.js` *(empty)* | **The story state machine goes here.** |
| `js/player.js` *(empty)* | **Stores, sleepfire, rest/food, verbs.** |

The single biggest scope win available: **`map.js` already renders a hand-inked chart.**
The Scene 12 plan board and the Scene 7 cave wall are both *that renderer with different
marks on it*. No new art pipeline, and it looks intentional because it matches what's
already there.

## 7. The Buildable Cut — the actual story, scene by scene

Thirteen scenes. This is the shippable game, written out at the level of detail you'd
build from. Where a number appears, it's a starting value to tune, not a law.

**New entries needed in `ISLANDS` (`js/world.js`):**

```js
// The hub — far southwest, past the known ring.
{ name: "Hollow Stack", x: -3900, z: 3800, r: 420, h: 300, cliff: 0.34 },

// The rig's three stacks — far northeast. Iron gets strung between them.
{ x: 4200, z: -3800, r: 130, h: 340, cliff: 0.11 },
{ x: 4450, z: -3950, r: 110, h: 300, cliff: 0.10 },
{ x: 4300, z: -3620, r: 145, h: 380, cliff: 0.12 },
```

Berk sits at `(0, -1100)`. The known ring runs out around radius 4600. Hub to rig is about
11,000 units — measure your actual cruise speed and tune the stack positions so the commute
lands near two minutes. It should feel like a journey and not like a chore.

---

### B1 — Prologue *(ink stills, ~50s, no input except advance)*

Six frames, chart-ink style, slow crossfades, no text, no voice. Wind and hearth crackle.

1. The room. Embers. Two sleeping shapes, one large, one small.
2. The saddle on its stand. Close. Dust on the seat.
3. The prototype fin in the corner, half under a bench.
4. Toothless's head beside sleeping Hiccup. Ears back.
5. The shutter pushed open. Sea, dark, nothing.
6. Toothless curled on the warm slab. Eyes open.

Frame 6 holds until the player presses anything. That hold is the scene.

**Cut from bible:** the walkable interior, the object-inspection verbs, the three flyable
memory fragments. The stills carry the same beats at a fraction of the cost.

---

### B2 — First Solo *(playable, ~3 min, morning, spawn at Berk)*

Fades up already airborne off the cliff behind the house, low and dropping.

**The fin is the tutorial.** No handling changes are needed — the existing model, flown
solo, already overcorrects. Just don't help the player. No control hints beyond the
existing HUD legend.

- **Objective text:** none for the first 60 seconds.
- Then: *Fly.*

**Ambient life:** `setupFlights(..., spawn)` already puts two flocks across the player's
line of sight at spawn. Keep that. It's the last time he sees dragons living normally, and
it costs nothing because it's already written.

**Exit:** flying past radius 3000 from origin.

---

### B3 — Beyond the Chart *(playable, ~4 min, day → dusk)*

Open water. The scene is the emptiness.

- At radius **4600**, the soft boundary. No dialog, no confirm. Three things change:
  the named islands stop, the water tint shifts colder, and **the chart (`Tab`) goes blank
  past the ring** — `map.js` renders only what's inside the known radius.
- **One prop:** a drifting spar with fittings in the wrong colour — a dull blue-grey. It is
  scenery, not a pickup, and most players will fly straight past it. That's fine; it pays
  off in B6 when they see the colour again on the shelf.
- Weather degrades with distance: existing fog density and a colder sky tint.

**Exit:** crossing radius 5200 heading northeast, or five minutes elapsed, whichever first.
Then a hard cut to night.

---

### B4 — The Rig *(playable, ~6 min, night, first stealth)*

**Audio before visual.** Hammering and a low industrial rumble on approach, then the
furnace glow on the underside of the cloud, then the thing itself.

**Layout across the three stacks:**

| Element | Where |
|---|---|
| Smelter | Stack 2, the tall one. Constant orange light, the loudest thing on the map. |
| Cage tier | Stack 1, three blocks of three cages under a roof of spars |
| Dock | Stack 3, waterline, jetty and cranes |
| Spine | Iron walkways strung between all three, 40–90 units above the water |

**Beat 4a — the pass.** Scripted approach vector, low, along the full length. The player has
control; the point is that it takes eleven seconds to fly past and it does not end.

**Beat 4b — the tier.** Perch on a spar over the cages. Nine dragons, muzzled — reuse the
existing `dragon.glb`, static, one shared idle. A long look with **no interaction
available.**

**Beat 4c — the failure.** Plasma blast on a cage bar is the only verb offered.

It flares and does nothing. A faint scorch. **Then the lanterns turn.**

**Beat 4d — the escape.** Alert state, lanterns converging, a genuinely tense flight out.
No combat. The alloy fragment is picked up automatically on the way — no prompt, it just
appears in the corner of the HUD afterwards, which is more interesting than a pickup.

**Exit:** radius 6000 from the rig.

---

### B5 — Hollow Stack *(playable, ~5 min, night → day)*

He picks the island. **Offer three** — Hollow Stack plus two of the existing outer sea
stacks. The choice is real and permanent and there is no wrong answer.

**Shelter:** no build minigame. Four deadfall logs scattered on the island, dragged to the
overhang with a grab-and-pull verb. On the fourth, the shelter mesh swaps in — mouth to the
sea, a sleeping stone, a flat shelf, and a wall.

**Sleeping here from now on:** saves, advances the day, restores rest.

**Cut from bible:** the rock-blasting, the hollow-scraping, the arranging. Four logs and a
mesh swap gets you the same feeling of having made a place.

---

### B6 — The Lab *(playable, 20–40 min, one full in-game day)*

The alloy fragment goes on the shelf. DOM overlay in `map.js`'s ink styling — same
parchment, same hand.

**3 fires × 4 materials × 3 conditions = 36 combinations. Every result authored.**

Fires: **Plasma Burst** · **Sustained Plasma** · **Low Smoulder**
Materials: **Alloy** · **Iron** · **Rope** · **Sailcloth**
Conditions: **Cold** · **Pre-heated** · **Soaked**

| | Cold | Pre-heated | Soaked |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Burst → Alloy** | Nothing. Faint scorch. | Nothing. Faint scorch. | Nothing. Steam. |
| **Sustained → Alloy** | Glows dull red. Cools unchanged. | **Dents. Colour blooms blue-gold.** | Glows, steams, unchanged. |
| **Smoulder → Alloy** | Nothing. | Nothing. | Nothing. |
| **Burst → Iron** | Scars deep. Metal splashes. | Punches through. | Scars, spits. |
| **Sustained → Iron** | Glows, softens, sags. | **Cuts through cleanly.** | Glows, sags slower. |
| **Smoulder → Iron** | Warms only. | Warms. | Nothing. |
| **Burst → Rope** | Severs instantly. | Severs. | Frays, holds. |
| **Sustained → Rope** | Burns through. | Burns through fast. | Steams, then burns. |
| **Smoulder → Rope** | Chars slowly through. | Burns through. | Nothing. |
| **Burst → Sailcloth** | Blows a hole, ignites. | Ignites. | Tears, no flame. |
| **Sustained → Sailcloth** | Gone in seconds. | Gone. | Burns after steaming. |
| **Smoulder → Sailcloth** | Smoulders, spreads. | Catches. | Nothing. |

**The tempting wrong answer** is the bolded alloy cell: pre-heat, then sustained plasma. It
is the *only* alloy cell in the entire grid that produces a visible change. Players will
pour a night into iterating on it. Give the dent a repeat mechanic — hit it again, it dents
slightly more — that plateaus hard after four attempts and never breaks through.

**The real finding** isn't "how to cut alloy." It's the comparison: *sustained plasma cuts
iron clean and cannot touch this.* The wall makes that legible because the iron row sits
right under the alloy row. That's a genuine deduction the player makes by looking at their
own notes, and it earns the failure.

**The wall persists** in `localStorage`. Failures never clear.

**Exit:** when the player has run 20+ combinations *and* attempted the pre-heat line at
least three times, the only remaining verb becomes *sleep*. Don't force it — remove the
alternatives and let them arrive at it.

---

### B7 — The Dream *(playable, ~90s)*

**Cheap in three.js and the best scene in the build.** It is less geometry, not more.

- Terrain, water, sky, HUD, chart: all off.
- `FogExp2`, density ~0.06, warm dark red.
- One point light, intensity ramping 0 → 40 across the full 90 seconds, positioned ahead
  and slowly closing.
- Controls: damping ×0.25, thrust inverted to feel like *pressure* rather than speed.
  Wrong on purpose.
- Three billboard sprites drift past at 20s, 45s, 70s: a hatchling silhouette *(a hint —
  he hasn't met her yet)*, the furnace, a pair of hands.
- At 90s: light floods to white, all audio cuts, one second of silence, black.

No fail state. No objective. The player cannot do it wrong.

---

### B8 — The Wake *(4 ink stills, ~20s)* + Fire *(playable, ~6 min)*

**Stills:**

1. The shelf, scorched black in a wide splash.
2. The sample, turned over in his claws.
3. His eye to the hole.
4. Sun through the hole.

**Then the skill.** Hold the fire input — long, with rising unstable feedback (screen
shake, audio pitch climb, a wobble that the player has to ride rather than ignore).

- **Attempt 1:** fails. He gags. Nothing.
- **Attempt 2:** fails. Released too early.
- **Attempt 3+:** succeeds if held through the unstable window. A short, brutally hot gout.
  It scorches the rock and **drops him on his side.**
- **Attempt 4 immediately after:** fails outright. He's spent.

That failure is the lesson, and it hands the player straight to B9 wanting food, without a
quest telling them to want it.

---

### B9 — The Hunt *(playable, ~5 min, day)*

**Stores: `Fed` / `Thin` / `Empty`.** Displayed as one small ink glyph, no bar.

- Fish shoals spawn in shallow water inside a radius of the hub — visible as surface
  disturbance from altitude.
- **The verb:** dive, level out in a narrow window near the surface, snap. The existing
  flight model does all the work; the whole implementation is a trigger volume and a
  timing check.
- Each fish moves you one step up, capped at Fed.
- Sleeping restores rest but not food.

**Sleepfire costs one step.** Fed → Thin → Empty. **Firing on Empty succeeds but leaves him
grounded and unable to fly for roughly thirty seconds.** Remember that; B12 is built on it.

Let this scene be pleasant. Good weather, good water. It's the only time in the game he
looks content.

---

### B10 — Sigrún *(playable, ~10 min, day → night)*

A cry across the water while he's eating.

**10a — the flight.** Follow sound. No marker on the chart. The audio should be losable, so
the player has to circle and re-acquire.

**10b — what he finds.** A grounded adult on a stack a long way out — `dragon.glb` scaled
up ~1.4× and tinted, one wing held wrong. A hatchling beside her; `makeDragon`'s `isBaby`
path already exists in `flights.js`.

**Dress the stack, not the dragon.** Torn scrub, a fresh rockfall scar, a nest half blown
apart. A storm did this. There is no rope on this island, no net, no iron, and no people.
She has nothing to do with the rig and the environment is the only place we say so.

**10c — approach.** Three dragon-speech verbs on the d-pad: **chirp**, **roar**,
**head-press**. Roar is available. Roaring here makes her rear and drives him back thirty
units — a real, recoverable mistake the player is likely to make once.

**10d — fish.** Three deliveries. Deliberately repetitive. He does it, gets nothing back,
and leaves.

**10e — the purge.** Camped at a distance that night, the hatchling sleep-purges. A puff.
Harmless. **She does it three times over about forty seconds** while the player watches
with nothing else to do.

That's the answer to *why has no dragon ever noticed this* — hatchlings do it small and
often and in the open, adults do it enormously and privately onto their own fireproof hide.

**10f — leaving.** Morning. No thank-you. No companion joins. No quest complete.

---

### B11 — The Plan *(playable, ~8 min)*

**This is `map.js` with an annotation layer**, which is the single biggest scope win in the
project — the parchment renderer, the ink, the coastline tracing all already exist.

**What recon in B4 recorded into state:**

- Brazier positions (12)
- Patrol lantern paths on the tier (8) and the spine (4)
- The three cage blocks
- Wind direction
- The dock

**What the player does:** drags marks onto the chart, sets an **entry point**, orders the
**three cage blocks** 1-2-3, and draws an **exit route**. What they build configures the
B12 encounter for real — entry position, which block loads first, where the exit lights up.

**Optional recon flights.** Fly back out, observe more, improve the board. Voluntary.
Skipping it means fewer braziers marked, which means a darker, riskier entry.

**The plan is wrong in exactly one way.** A supply ship is in at the dock on the night of
the raid. **It is not observable during recon and it is not in state until B12 loads it.**
It puts **six extra lanterns on the lower spine**, awake and moving, where the board says
dark.

Not a gotcha. The world is bigger than your information about it. This is what raises the
alarm — not the hatchling.

---

### B12 — The Raid *(playable, 20–30 min, night — the game)*

**The economy, which is the entire design:**

- **Nine cages, in three blocks of three.**
- **Sleepfire opens a block**, not a cage.
- The player arrives **Fed**. That's **two sleepfires.**
- **Two blocks. Six dragons. Three cages left.**

The player cannot afford the third block, and they will work that out somewhere in the
middle of block two, which is exactly when it should land.

**Guards:** lantern-cones on splines. No character models.

- Cone: 55°, range 90 units, **1.2 seconds of continuous line of sight** to trigger alert
- Tail-strike from behind, within 30° of their heading: **lantern goes out permanently**
- Alert: nearest three lanterns converge, 25 seconds, resets to patrol if unseen
- Wing-gust snuffs braziers at ~25 units; **a walker relights them after 40 seconds**
- Every sleepfire triggers an alert automatically. It is loud and it is bright and there is
  no stealth version of it.

**Phase 1 — Entry.** Silent glide from height into the player's chosen entry. Powered
flight is audible inside the rig; managing altitude is managing noise.

**Phase 2 — Blocks one and two.** Sleepfire, alert, break line of sight, wait it out,
repeat. Freed dragons become `flights.js` flocks with an added exit waypoint — that
waypoint mode is genuine new work in `flights.js`, budget for it.

**Phase 3 — The dock.** The player's route runs through it because that's the route they
drew. Six lanterns that aren't on the board. **Alarm.**

---

### B12b — The Choice

**The exit lights up.** Generously, unambiguously, in the ink language the game has used
for every objective so far. It is clean, it is open, and **it genuinely works** — a player
who takes it flies out safely.

**Block three is behind him, with a hatchling in it, screaming.**

**He is on `Empty`.**

So the player knows, mechanically and precisely, what going back costs: firing on Empty
grounds him for thirty seconds in the middle of an alerted fortress. **The game has taught
them this in B9 and they will remember it.**

There is no timer. No cutscene takes the controls.

**If the player flies out:** at the mouth of the exit, Toothless turns around anyway, out of
their hands. The one moment in the game where he overrides you. It should be shocking, and
it should not be softened.

**He opens block three. He fires on Empty. It works, barely, and he goes down.**

They take him with nets while he's on the deck, unable to lift. Not a boss fight —
industrial equipment doing exactly what it was built for. **He is caught because of a
choice he made, priced in a currency the game taught him.**

**Sigrún is a shape in the sky above the rig.** No cut to her. No music sting. Second
playthrough only.

### B12c — The Cage *(playable, ~90s, confined)*

**Not a cutscene. The player is in the cage.**

Camera locked close. Alloy bars. Muzzle on. Available verbs: **thrash**, **bite the lock**,
and nothing. All fail. Sleepfire is greyed out — Empty, and muzzled.

**Let it run slightly too long.** Through the bars, in the dark: shapes moving. Shouting.
A lantern goes out. Then another. Then a third.

### B12d — Sigrún *(4 ink stills, ~20s)*

1. The bars, from inside, looking up.
2. Four wings across the moon. *This is the shot the species exists for.*
3. The muzzle torn off — not gently.
4. Her face. Unimpressed.

### B12e — The Last Block *(playable, ~5 min, co-op finale)*

Systems change for two:

- **She carries out the dragons that can't fly.** He can't. Watch it happen and the
  division of labour is explained without a word.
- **Her sleepfire spirals and clings** where his drills. She uses it on the block-three
  lock housing and it *jams it shut behind them* — the first key acquisition in the game,
  demonstrated a full act before it's ever explained.
- **Guards who see her run.** The player can herd lanterns with her.

**It's a run, not a fight.** Out over the water, the rig going dark behind them — smelter
doused, walkways down, and the crews visibly getting into boats and being fine. Broken,
not destroyed. He never wins a fight in this mission.

### B13 — After *(playable, ~3 min, dawn, then 4 stills)*

Hollow Stack. Freed dragons on the beach — reuse `flights.js` flocks in a grounded idle
state. Sigrún on a rock, wing still bad. Hatchlings asleep in a heap.

The player can walk among them. Nothing is required. Some let him near; some don't.

**Stills:**

1. The beach from above. More dragons than the island is meant to hold.
2. Him walking through them.
3. The sample with the hole in it, hung on the wall. One claw-scratch beside it.
4. The wall, wide. Mostly empty.

**End.**

| The dream, walkable surreal space | **Keep it, simplified.** Fog, no terrain, no HUD, inverted-heavy controls, one light source that grows. This is *cheap in three.js* — it's less geometry, not more. Highest emotional return per hour of work in the entire project. |
| Guard NPCs with patrol AI | **Lantern-cones on fixed spline paths.** No character models — guards are moving lights with a vision cone. A tail-strike snuffs a lantern permanently. Reads perfectly at night from the air. |

| Cage-full-of-dragon-models | Low-poly reuse of the existing dragon GLB, static, one shared animation. Ten of them is fine. |

## 9. Build order

Each milestone is playable end-to-end before the next starts.

**M0 — The spine** *(`js/game.js`, `js/player.js`)*
Story state machine. Named beats, transitions, a save in `localStorage`. Objective text in
the HUD. Debug commands to jump to any scene. Nothing visible changes yet; everything
after this depends on it.

**M1 — Time and home**
Make `SUN_ELEVATION`/`SUN_AZIMUTH` in `world.js` dynamic. Day/night with a real night that
is *dark* and readable. Then: the hub island — pick one existing island, place a shelter,
make sleeping there advance the day and save.

**M2 — Stores and the hunt**
Fed/Thin/Empty. Fish spawns in specific water. Dive-and-catch verb. Sleep restores rest.
No story yet, but the economy runs.

**M3 — The lab**
DOM overlay in chart ink. 36 combinations, every result authored. The wall persists. The
tempting wrong answer (pre-heat + sustained plasma → visible dent, plateaus) is in from day
one. This is a *writing* task more than a code task.

**M4 — Sleepfire**
The dream sequence. The wake. The hold-to-charge input with unstable feedback. Cost
against stores. Exhaustion. Ties M2 and M3 together and is the moment the game becomes
itself.

**M5 — The rig**
Modular iron kit — walkways, spars, cage frames, smelter glow — assembled across three sea
stacks added to `ISLANDS`. Lantern-cone guards on splines. Detection and alert states.
Cages that only open to sleepfire. The scariest milestone; budget accordingly.

**M6 — Recon and the plan board**
Recon flights record observations into state. `map.js` gains an annotation layer. The
player drags marks and draws a route. The route configures the M5 encounter. The supply
ship is a hardcoded, invisible-to-recon variable that changes the dock's guard count.

**M7 — Sigrún**
Distress-cry encounter, fish deliveries, the hatchling's visible purge. Her appearance at
the rig. The cage sequence — locked camera, restricted verbs, ~90 seconds. The co-op
finale using `flights.js` for her flight behaviour and one scripted assist.

**M8 — The choice**
The exit lights up. The last cage. The override if the player flies out. This is 200 lines
of state machine and it's the reason the whole thing exists — build it *last* so everything
it depends on is solid, but design it *first* so everything points at it.

**M9 — Prologue, cutscenes, epilogue**
Ink stills. Three cutscenes plus the prologue and the closing. Ship.

## 10. Cut lines if time runs out

In order of what to drop first:

1. The plan board becomes three preset routes instead of a drag-and-drop board
2. The lab shrinks to 2 fires × 3 materials × 2 conditions
3. Recon flights are removed; the board comes pre-filled from Scene 5
4. The co-op finale becomes a single scripted escape run
5. The prologue becomes a title card

**Never cut:** the dream, the wake, and the choice. Those three are the game.

## 11. Decisions on record

All five closed. Recorded here so they don't get relitigated.

1. **Hiccup does not appear mid-game.** Prologue and epilogue only. No search party, no
   sighting, no cameo in Act II. The absence is the point and one appearance would spend it.
2. **Sigrún is an ordinary dragon with no connection to the rig.** Storm-broken wing,
   hungry hatchling, no grievance, no stake. She follows him to the rig uninvited, leaving
   the hatchling alone to do it. **After the rescue she becomes a permanent partner** —
   the game is a two-hander from Mission 2, and his arc is "learn to work with someone,"
   not "learn to lead."
3. **Nobody dies. Kids can play this.** See §0.5. The tail-strike is a non-lethal
   knockout, the rig stops rather than burns, crews are shown safe in boats, and Halvard
   loses his industry rather than his life. Mission 3's cost is a dragon *retaken*, seen
   again in a cage two missions later. What we don't soften: loneliness, failure, capture,
   and damage that doesn't fully mend.
4. **Bellows is sick from never sleeping**, not from chains — decades at smelting heat
   means never dropping deep enough to purge. Halvard is retiring them, kindly, with a
   replacement already chosen. **The finale is convincing them it's allowed to stop.** They
   survive; they never smelt or fly properly again; they live on Hollow Stack.
5. **Name pass: done now, as a table.** See §0.3. Ships as *Hush*, *Nettle*,
   *Skerryhold*, *the Ledger*, *Nightwake*, *Galeshear*, *Kilnback*, *Bellows*. Prose keeps
   the borrowed names for readability; the table is the single point of change.

6. **Eyvi grows.** A scale value per act, plus a wing-competence value that drives how
   badly she flies. She is a scrap in B10, awkward and airborne by the mirror mission, and
   in the epilogue she is flying badly around an island she owns. Cheap to implement,
   and she's the clock that shows how long all this took.
7. **Halvard appears exactly once**, in the buildable cut, at the capture. He walks over
   while Toothless is netted on the deck, unhurried, and looks at him with delight. No
   words, no fight, and he is gone before the cage door. **The orb-and-question-mark
   placeholder makes this nearly free**, and one appearance is worth more than none — an
   antagonist who is only ever an abstraction can't be *disappointed in you*.

### Placeholder art rule *(buildable cut)*

**Every human and every non-player dragon is a ball of light with a question mark in it.**
Not a stand-in to be embarrassed about — a deliberate, consistent, good-looking convention
held across the whole build. Warm light for the ones who mean you well, cold for the ones
who don't, and they get scale, motion, and light-colour to act with. Toothless is the only
thing in the game with a body.
